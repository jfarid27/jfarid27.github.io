I'm a software engineer and consultant with a career spent at the interface between business, customers, and computers. Most of my work has been end-to-end product engineering: understanding the problem, shaping the system, shipping it, and staying around long enough to see whether it actually worked.

I studied at Rutgers University and have been writing software professionally ever since — across early-stage startups, product teams, and independent consulting engagements. Over time I found that my real specialty is developing solutions rather than features: the ambiguous, cross-cutting problems that don't arrive with a ticket attached.

What I do

Today my work is a mix of engineering, applied statistics, and strategy. Through Lunamonta Technologies, I consult with early-stage teams on decentralized finance, data operations, and product delivery. The common thread is helping people make better decisions with the systems and data they already have.

How I work

I lean toward data-oriented, iterative approaches: instrument first, form a hypothesis, ship something small, and let the results argue for themselves. I'm equally happy deep in a type system or a statistical model, and I care about code that makes the right thing the easy thing.

Lately that has meant working with Effect and TypeScript, modeling risk for crypto lending markets, and writing about the craft of software on this site. My essays tend to start as notes to myself; publishing them is a way of finishing the thought.
